# LootFall: the world

The world exists to explain the game. Every mechanic should have a reason you could tell a stranger in a tavern, and
every reason should make you want to open one more chest. This is the reference for anything that speaks, sells, falls
out of a chest or happens in a scene; `docs/design.md` has the rules and the numbers.

## The premise in four answers

**Why is the world full of chests?** Three hundred years ago the demon of avarice, **Avarax, the Gilded Maw**, ruled
the realm of Aldmark through its treasure: every loose coin was his eye, every unsheathed blade his hand. King **Aldric
the Wise** and the archmages of the **Keyhold** worked **the Locking**: every treasure in the realm, from a farmer's
savings to the crown jewels, was shut into a warded chest. Blind and starving, the Maw was chained beneath the capital
behind the **Gilded Gate**. The chests never went away. Three centuries on they're everywhere: in attics and
barrows, on old battlefields, in goblin warrens, abbey crypts and noble strongrooms. Folk trade them unopened, the way
they'd trade a cow.

**Why sell them instead of opening them?** A Locking chest can't be seen into, scried or smashed: the ward turns any
blade and any spell. It opens for one kind of person only, a **Keywright** (someone born with the Key-mark), and only
once the chest is rightfully theirs: bought fairly or freely given, never stolen. So everyone else sells. The ward lets
one thing through: sound. Everybody shakes a chest before they sell it, and everybody guesses.

**Why can you open them?** You're one of Bram's kin, and the Key-mark runs in the family. Uncle Bram opened chests at
the Brindle crossroads for forty years until his mark faded with age; now it shows on your hand, and he hands you his
stall. You buy a chest, it's yours, the ward lets go, and you pick the old lock underneath.

**What does opening them accomplish?** For the folk who sell to you: coin in a world that's short of it. For the realm:
treasure back in the light, three hundred years late. For the Maw: every chest opened loosens the Locking a little.
That's what nobody tells you at first. The **Gilded Guild**, which licenses Keywrights and takes a weekly tithe "to keep
the Gate", wants you opening chests as fast as you can, and wants its tithe in gold. Gold you can see.

## The places (the shop's tiers)

Progress is a journey inward, from the edge of the realm to the Gate at its heart. Each move brings new people, new
things to hear, and new trouble.

| Tier | Place | Who comes | New to hear and do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brindle Crossroads**, Bram's stall where the north road meets the river | villagers, an innkeeper, young adventurers back from the Barrow | coin, arms, garb; the three strengths; haggling, naming a price |
| 2 | **Thornwick Market**, a market town on the edge of the Old Wood | a goblin, a huntress, a druid, a priest, a smuggler | potions and scrolls; locks; false bottoms and the drop; the display shelf |
| 3 | **A shop in Crownhold**, the capital | an archmage and his apprentice, a dwarf miner, a noblewoman, a knight, a pawnbroker, a mudlark | gems; two or three of a kind; sand; enchantment and the witchlight; the shop's name (synergies) |
| 4 | **The Royal Emporium**, in the palace's shadow, by the Gilded Gate | demons through the cracking Gate, a court lady, the King in disguise, and once in a while the Maw himself | demon gold (iron: the lodestone silences it); hurried, greedy and bargaining customers |

Each move costs the renovation's price: "Move to Thornwick", "Move to Crownhold", "The Royal Emporium".

## The people of the story

- **Uncle Bram**: an old Keywright whose mark has faded. Warm, proud, a little ashamed of the debt. Your mentor.
- **Mister Vane**: Collector of the **Gilded Guild**. Polite, pale, patient, and the Maw's.
- **Mara, the Hooded Woman**: the last archmage of the Keyhold. She's been hunting the Aldric Key's shards for twenty
  years.
- **Nim the Peddler**: a horned, cheerful half-something who sells **curios**, oddments that come out of Locking chests
  and bend the rules of the trade. Nobody asks what Nim is.
- **The Stranger**: brings a chest wrapped in iron chains, so nothing inside can move. Never says where from.
- **King Aldric IX**: old, tired, worried about the Gate. Calls himself Old Aldo, and sells you chests from the Royal
  Treasury to see if you're the Keywright the realm needs.
- **Avarax, the Gilded Maw**: the demon of greed. Weak enough to need the Guild, strong enough to visit the Emporium in
  a borrowed body dripping with rings.

## Things (the kinds)

Six kinds of things, each with the sound you'd expect from it and the strength of shake it takes to stir it. The world
made them; the rules didn't change.

| Kind | Sound | Stirs at | Notes | Common to legendary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coin | jingle | gentle | delicate | Copper Pennies, Silver Marks, Gold Crowns, Old Sovereigns, Dragon Gold |
| Arms | clank | firm | loud; iron (the lodestone holds it) | Rusty Dagger, Soldier's Helm, Steel Longsword, Knight's Gauntlet, Runeblade |
| Garb | flump | hard | soft | Moth-Eaten Cloak, Hunter's Coat, Silk Robe, Archmage's Mantle, Royal Ermine |
| Potions | slosh | gentle | delicate; glass, breaks if dropped (from Thornwick) | Swamp Tonic, Healing Draught, Elixir of Vigour, Phoenix Tears, Ambrosia |
| Scrolls | rustle | hard | soft (from Thornwick) | Tattered Map, Spell Scroll, Bestiary, Grimoire, Locking Codex |
| Gems | tinkle | gentle | delicate (from Crownhold) | Brass Ring, Silver Amulet, Ruby Ring, Starstone, Dragon's Eye |

Fakes: **Demon Gold** (gilded iron coins and gems: the lodestone silences them), **Paste Gems**, and **scroll cases
full of sand** (sand pours at a gentle shake; parchment only rustles at a hard one). Anything can be **enchanted**
(the witchlight makes it glow when it sounds).

Every seller has one way of judging their chest (which decides their price, and what they miss), one rule that changes
what you can do or what the deal is, a place their chests come from, and a story they tell. What they say they heard
is still on the slip when they listened, but the stories are about where the chest came from and what happened to
them; how far to believe them is part of the job.

## Why the rules are true here

- *A thing only sounds once the shake stirs it; clanks drown out delicate things.* The ward lets sound through and
  nothing else; things inside move and sound the way they would in any box.
- *Sellers price only what they think is there.* Nobody but a Keywright has ever seen inside one.
- *Locks.* The ward lifts once the chest is yours; the old iron lock underneath doesn't. Pick it cleanly and nothing's
  scratched; force it and glass breaks.
- *False bottoms.* People hid their best things under a floor when the Wardens came round with the Locking: the
  hollow boom of a drop is the only way to know.
- *Enchantment.* Things the archmages enchanted before they were locked stay enchanted; the witchlight shows it.
- *The lodestone.* Iron can't move near it. Arms go quiet, and so does demon gold, which is iron under the gilt.
- *Curios.* Oddments from the Locking that bend a rule of the trade. Nim finds them; nobody asks where.
- *Bram's stash and his word.* Old Keywrights keep a little by, and the Guild still takes Bram's word, once.
- *A rotten chest.* The ward holds a lid shut for ever; it doesn't stop the wood rotting. Barrow damp gets into
  everything, and a chest from there stands about two seconds of shaking before it starts to come apart.
- *A goblin's fee.* Goblins charge for everything, including looking.
- *A wager, an heirloom, a devil's due.* An archmage who remembers the Locking likes to see the craft done well; a
  knight's grandfather's sword is his whatever chest it's in; a devil never makes a bargain he doesn't win something by.
- *The King's game.* Aldric IX needs a Keywright he can trust with the Crown Chest, so he tests them the only way that
  counts: name what's inside. He comes by one day in four or so, the Maw one day in ten.
- *The Maw's gaze.* Greed is catching. Take his coin, and everyone in the city wants more today.
