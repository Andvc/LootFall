# Inked 2D cut-outs carry LootFall's endless loot

LootFall should stay a 2D game and trade its low-resolution pixel art for a high-resolution **inked cel style**: dark-outlined, flat-shaded actors and loot lit from the top left, set against desaturated, low-contrast painted parallax backdrops, with the knight fighting on his own in the side-view lane he already has. Torchlight, Diablo III and Hades each reached this crisp-foreground, soft-background split independently, and it matches what AI generation can do in late 2026: frontier image models make near-professional single illustrations and icons, but large sets still drift, animation frames still flicker, generated 9-slice frames don't hold their geometry, AI effect flipbooks remain unproven, and AI-made 3D meshes still need topology, delighting and non-humanoid rigging work that this project's cloud-session workflow can't do. The framework therefore asks AI only for neutral, static stills built in families (a base design plus material and biome variants) and puts everything players must read the same way every time into code: the twelve rarity treatments, UI frames, VFX motion, numbers and all animation, which comes from a code-built cut-out rig for the knight and procedural motion presets for the monsters. Combat stays automatic and is shown through four presentation modes keyed to time-to-kill (duel, wave, sweep, leap) under a fixed on-screen budget, with boss gates always played in full; the loot reads through layered icons, a two-level armoury, Path of Exile's tree grammar and a codex that aggregates its 114,432 entries instead of listing them. 3D lost because monster variety, not the hero, drives its cost; pixel art lost because it leaves too few pixels for twelve tiers and visible gear, breaks under rotation-based animation, and sits in Steam's most crowded lane of AI-disclosed idle games. No public source measures cleanup hours per asset, so a timed pilot should fix the budget before production, while GitHub-hosted packs (Ancient Beast and Valyria Tear creatures and backdrops, OpenMoji and game-icons items, Kenney particles, KayKit for a 3D comparison) stage every layer of the look in the meantime.

## AI art in late 2026 nails single stills and still breaks on sets and motion

The frontier image models of October 2026 compete on consistency, not raw quality. OpenAI's **gpt-image-2**, released on 21 April 2026, renders up to 2K with a planning mode and up to eight variants per prompt ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/openai-launches-chatgpt-images-2-0-with-2k-output-and-reasoning-mode/)); Google's Nano Banana Pro is sold on holding a subject steady across reference images, with guides telling users to build a reference sheet first ([Selfielab](https://selfielabstudio.com/blog/nano-banana-pro-perfect-character-consistency-guide-20260212)); **FLUX.2** takes up to ten references and ships an open-weight checkpoint ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-2)); Seedream 5.0 claims fourteen ([HowAIWorks](https://howaiworks.ai/models/seedream)); and Midjourney V8 has style and omni references but no official API ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/midjourney-v8-rolls-out-with-5x-faster-generation-but-charges-4x-more-for-its-best-features/); [Ludo](https://ludo.ai/compare/best-ai-image-generators-for-game-art)). Characters follow the same pattern: supply references and identity holds across a series of edits, by Black Forest Labs' account ([BFL docs](https://docs.bfl.ai/guides/usecases_editing_character_consistency)). When a whole market advertises multi-reference consistency, single-image quality has stopped being the bottleneck, and shipped failures agree: reviewers of Vapor World: Over The Mind fixed on its inconsistent AI character models until the studio pulled the AI cutscenes ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-a-touch-of-ai-is-all-it-takes-to-trigger-backlash-as-a-promising-new-indie-falls-afoul-of-slop-skeptics/)), and Codex Mortis's demo was called "riddled with AI artifacts" ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/is-the-first-100-percent-ai-video-game-the-ultimate-rage-bait)).

Sets of hundreds hold together only when everything the generator could vary is locked and then enforced after generation. A vendor's own example shows an unanchored icon set drifting by about the **30th icon**: the light moves from top-left to overhead, outlines thicken, and poison becomes hard to tell from fire ([Layer](https://layer.ai/use-cases/game-icon-sets)). The remedies in use are an anchor image that serves as the reference for every item, whole sets generated on one sheet ("thirty icons cost one generation instead of thirty") ([SpriteCook](https://www.spritecook.ai/game-icon-generator)), sketch-guided image-to-image at low influence and custom models trained on **10 to 30** in-style images ([Scenario](https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/designing-icons-for-a-match-3-game)), followed by deterministic cleanup. No source measures how consistency falls with set size, and nearly all of this guidance comes from vendors. Transparency is the quiet trap: gpt-image-2 now rejects transparent backgrounds, which a reseller dates to a switch-off on 30 September 2026 ([OpenAI Developer Community](https://community.openai.com/t/is-transparent-background-support-broken-in-gpt-image-2/1402707); [APIYI](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/live/2026-10/gpt-image-2-transparent-shutdown)), and the Nano Banana models paint a fake checkerboard instead of alpha. The fix that survives any provider change is to render on a flat key colour, or once on white and once on black and compute alpha from the difference ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/jide/nano-banana-2-transparent)).

Motion is still the weak spot. PixelLab's automatic animation needs a canvas of **64x64 or smaller** ([PixelLab docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/create-animations-automatic)), Ludo's sprite animation exports 64 to 256 px cells ([Ludo](https://ludo.ai/features/sprite-generator)), and video models close a loop from first and last frames largely because little happens in such clips ([Mervin Praison](https://mer.vin/2026/09/which-ai-video-models-actually-take-a-first-and-last-frame/)). Each diffusion frame differs slightly, so flicker comes with the method ([Topaz Labs community](https://community.topazlabs.com/t/deflicker-for-ai-videos/42459)), and a developer who mined video for sprites still did "a lot of manual work, frame by frame" ([MP4 to Sprite](https://yewlark.itch.io/mp4tosprite)). Splitting a single painting into rig parts is the promising new route, and it is unproven: See-through decomposes one anime illustration into up to 23 inpainted layers ([GitHub](https://github.com/shitagaki-lab/see-through)), and rig-parts compiles those into a Spine rig, but it wants a front view with arms clear, invents **19 to 27%** of the visible pixels, and calls its ten finished characters "an existence proof, not a rate" ([GitHub](https://github.com/firejune/rig-parts)). No tool targets Godot's Skeleton2D, and none was tested on side views or non-humanoid creatures.

AI 3D has outgrown its detail problem but not its production problems. Meshy 7.1 offers 4096³ geometry ([Meshy changelog](https://docs.meshy.ai/zh/webapp/changelog)), Tripo's Smart Mesh P1 makes meshes of 48 to 20,000 faces in about ten seconds ([Tripo docs](https://developers.tripo3d.com/en/models/p1)), Rodin Gen-2 outputs 4k to 50k quads with T- or A-pose enforcement ([Scenario](https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/rodin-hyper3d-models-the-essentials/)), and the open TRELLIS.2 is MIT-licensed but needs a 24 GB GPU ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). What still fails is topology that deforms well, lighting baked into the colour map ("the most common production failure", per a vendor) and style across a set ([Neural4D](https://www.neural4d.com/how-tos/how-to-solve-baked-shadows-and-lighting-issues)); retopology destroys the UVs and forces a rebake ([Neural4D guide](https://blog.neural4d.com/user-guide/blender-retopology-ai-3d-models/)), and every source that discusses animated hero characters expects a cleanup pass ([Summer Engine](https://www.summerengine.com/blog/ai-3d-game-asset-generator)). Rigging splits by body plan. Humanoids are solved by free auto-riggers and clip libraries, but Mixamo excludes quadrupeds, wings and tails ([Tripo blog, citing Adobe](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/mixamo-alternative-that-can-rig-non-humanoid-or-quadruped)), creature riggers such as Tripo Rigging 2.5 are vendor claims nobody has tested independently ([Scenario](https://www.scenario.com/models/tripo-rigging-v25)), and text-to-motion is trained on people: Tencent's HY-Motion lists animals, seamless loops and in-place animations as out of scope ([GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HY-Motion-1.0)). The research found no shipped game built on AI-generated, AI-rigged, animated creatures. Generation money is trivial, from cents to under a dollar per attempt (Meshy charges 20 credits a generation ([Meshy pricing](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing)) and one Rodin API reseller $0.80 ([EmpirioLabs](https://docs.empiriolabs.ai/models/hyper3d-gen2))); the developer's hours are the cost, and nobody publishes them.

The remaining asset types sort cleanly. Painted backdrops are among AI's strongest outputs, and layer separation for parallax is the weaker step, handled by generating each layer on a key colour or by Qwen-Image-Layered's decomposition, which can't be told what goes into which layer ([GitHub](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image-Layered)). The only dedicated AI flipbook tool found is an Unreal plugin whose quality claims are the seller's ([Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/695f3e5d-3aae-4ed8-9720-a4f65f9fe4ac)). UI is weakest of all: panels and buttons generated one at a time come out with mismatched lighting, borders and corners, and 9-slice margins have to be marked by hand ([SpriteCook](https://www.spritecook.ai/blog/generate-game-ui-with-ai)).

| Asset type | Realistic ceiling, late 2026 | Typical failures | What LootFall should do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single illustrations (portraits, monsters, key art) | Near-professional per image | "AI look", hand and detail artefacts, drift across a cast | Generate with references; human paint-over for the knight, guardians and store art |
| Item icons, one at a time | High | Light, angle and outline drift; no alpha from some models | Locked frame, light and palette; key-colour matte in the repo |
| Icon sets in the hundreds | Moderate, mostly a review cost | Batch drift, sameness across slot types | Families plus scripted checks; rarity never in the art |
| Painted backdrops | High | Seams, perspective oddities | Generate the band, check tiling, clamp values |
| Parallax layers | Moderate | Poor separation, invented hidden areas | One generation per layer on a key colour |
| Frame-by-frame animation | Low to moderate, best at 64-256 px | Drift, flicker, loop and pivot jitter | Avoid; static art moved by code |
| Cut-out rigs from one painting | Emerging, front-facing humanoids only | Invented occluded parts, depth errors | Knight only, assembled and checked by hand |
| AI 3D models | High for rigid props, low for animated creatures | Topology, baked light, set consistency | Keep out of the shipped presentation |
| Auto-rigging and AI motion | Solved for humanoids, unproven otherwise | Non-humanoid rigs and motion fail | Not needed in 2D |
| VFX flipbooks | Low | Frame coherence, soft alpha | Particles and shaders in code; AI single frames on black |
| UI frames and 9-slices | Low to moderate | Uneven borders, edges that won't stretch | Draw in code; AI for ornament at most |

Visible AI art also carries a reception cost that the art direction has to manage. About **30.8% of 2026 Steam releases** to July disclosed generative AI, up from 10.9% in 2024 ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-take-cover-because-it-looks-like-more-than-half-of-steam-games-will-have-an-ai-disclosure-by-2027-2028/)). In Sulka Haro's census of 53,597 releases, AI-flagged games rate 4 to 6 points lower and **72% of the ones that failed used AI for visuals**, while the successes leaned more on AI voice and localization ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/steam-study-of-over-53-000-games-finds-60-90-percent-of-the-growth-in-monthly-releases-on-valves-store-is-from-games-using-ai-and-almost-none-of-them-make-money/)). Since January 2026 Valve requires disclosure of AI used in in-game content and store assets ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines)). Idle players are primed: PC Gamer counted 54 idle games released in one week, the vast majority with AI disclosures and many with "strikingly similar" interfaces ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-54-idle-games-released-on-steam-last-week-and-many-look-eerily-similar)), and when Galaxy Idle Clicker switched to AI-made art, 68% of its last 70 reviews turned negative ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-launch-New-free-to-play-idle-clicker-game-hits-Steam-faces-immediate-backlash-over-artwork.1081692.0.html)). The binary flag can't tell AI art from low effort, so these are correlations, but they put three things in the brief: a coherent look from launch, disclosure from day one, and a style that leaves AI little room to show its tells.

## Inked 2D over muted painted backdrops beats 3D, pixel art and painterly

The recommended look is a **two-layer composition**. The foreground (knight, monsters, chests, loot, effects) is inked cel: one uniform dark outline, flat fills with two or three shade steps lit from the top left, a short ramp per material, slightly chunky proportions with an oversized weapon and helm so gear reads at lane size, and one saturated focus colour per actor. The background is painterly, softer, and kept lower in value contrast and saturation than anything that moves. Three studios reached this split separately. Torchlight's art director paired "a painterly background with crisp, comic-book-ish style characters atop it" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2009-09-10-an-insight-into-the-art-of-torchlight.html)); Diablo III subordinated art to gameplay through "a layering system for any given moment in the game", with strong silhouettes and bold colour ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-diablo-iii-s-art-director-shows-off-design-process)); and Hades put 3D-rendered characters over mostly hand-painted environments ([Steam developer post](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1145360/discussions/0/1864993755183591660)), each character with one main focus colour over desaturated swatches ([style study](https://kymira.bearblog.dev/style-study-hades/)). LootFall needs the rule today: in the current build the Greenwood backdrop is as saturated as the knight and the slimes, and the reveal banner printed over it ("A LEGENDARY! Its power goes off in the fight.") nearly vanishes ([current screenshot](/home/user/lootfall/shots_open/demo_mythic_card.png)).

The style is chosen as much for what a script can enforce as for how it looks. Outline width, distance from the palette, light direction and framing are all measurable, so drift across thousands of generated stills can be caught and corrected automatically, whereas soft painterly rendering and photorealism give a checker nothing firm to hold. Darkest Dungeon's lone artist chose a style with "externalized rules" so he could art-direct himself, and kept "dead gray" and black out of everything but the inks ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/red-hook-studios-talks-about-the-creation-of-darkest-dungeon); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com:443/articles/the-gothic-sensibilities-of-darkest-dungeon/1100-6424880/)); with AI, rules like these become the prompt and the post-processing spec. The owner already runs this kind of rule at pixel scale in Keywright: materials on a shadow, mid and light ramp, lit from the top left, with a dark outline ([Keywright CLAUDE.md](/home/user/keywright/CLAUDE.md)). Flat fills also leave little rendered detail for a model to get wrong, which hides the artefacts reviewers name, and cel shading has a clarity pedigree: Nintendo said Wind Waker's let it present objects "in a more easy-to-understand way" ([Wikipedia: Cel shading](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Cel_shaded)).

3D buys three things LootFall can't collect cheaply. A real-time hero gets every gear combination free, since Godot's BoneAttachment3D makes a mesh follow a bone ([Godot class reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot/master/doc/classes/BoneAttachment3D.xml)); animation never drifts; humanoid clips are reusable. But 3D cost scales with body plans, the game needs many monsters across many biomes, and each needs a rig and clips, with wings, blobs and many-legged creatures exactly where AI rigging and motion fail. The cleanup (retopology, weight painting, rig repair) is interactive Blender work, while the owner works through cloud sessions that reach only GitHub ([CLAUDE.md](/home/user/lootfall/CLAUDE.md)). Pre-rendering 3D to sprites, the Dead Cells method ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/case-study-dead-cells-character-art-pipeline/)), cures frame drift but multiplies gear: every visible piece must be baked for every hero frame (an illustrative 300 items at 80 frames is about 24,000 images) and baked again after each change. Supergiant itself moved from Hades' pre-rendered frames ("a 104-frame animation rendered at 32 angles") to real-time 3D characters in Hades II ([Steam developer post](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1145360/discussions/0/1864993755183591660); [DigiPen](https://www.digipen.edu/showcase/news/hades-2-graphic-programmer)), which shows the answer depends on the project. LootFall's fixed side view decides it: the knight faces one way, so the angle multiplier that made Hades costly drops to one, and a 2D cut-out pays almost nothing per monster beyond its illustration. Effects cost the same on every route because they stay 2D overlays, so VFX argues neither way; whether Godot's GL Compatibility renderer handles many skinned, toon-shaded meshes well went unresearched.

Pixel art is the strongest alternative and deserves its case. LootFall already uses it; pixel-specific models such as Retro Diffusion generate on the grid with a fixed palette ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/blog/retro-diffusions-pixel-art-models-are-now-on-replicate)); MIT tools such as unfake.py and Sprite Fusion's Pixel Snapper repair general models' fake pixels automatically ([unfake.py](https://github.com/painebenjamin/unfake.py); [Pixel Snapper](https://github.com/Hugo-Dz/spritefusion-pixel-snapper)); and Vampire Survivors began on sprites from a bought asset pack ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/vampire-survivors-castlevania-nintendo-switch-stealing-claims-dlc)) and sold about 6.6 million Steam copies by one estimate ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1794680/Vampire_Survivors)). Three things count against it here. A sprite at today's 2x scale has too few pixels for ten worn slots and twelve tiers of material escalation. The animation an AI pipeline depends on, rotating, scaling and squashing static parts, breaks the pixel grid unless parts move in whole pixels at integer scales, the rule the owner already keeps in Keywright. And pixel-art idle games with AI disclosures are exactly what Steam is flooded with. The other styles each fail one test: a painterly foreground muddies values and resists normalising; HD-2D is "more expensive than people realize" ([Wikipedia: HD-2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD-2D)), and lit hi-bit pixel art makes every asset "answer to the technical needs of the dynamic lighting" ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/09/11/sea-of-stars-level-design-sabotage-studio-thierry-boulanger-interview/)); flat vector risks looking like a mobile ad, which the inked rules exist to prevent; and full chibi gacha styling risks reading as pay-to-win on PC, an inference from monetisation backlashes rather than a measured effect.

| Option | Clarity in a busy lane | Fit with AI output | Visible gear | Cost per new monster | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inked cel 2D, cut-out knight, procedural monsters | High (outline plus two-layer rule) | High: stills only, every rule checkable | Good: one part per bone, rarity tinted in code | One illustration | **Recommended** |
| Low-res pixel art (today) | High at small sizes | High for stills, low for animation | Limited by pixel count | One sprite; frames weak | Runner-up |
| Real-time toon 3D | High | High for props, low for creatures | Best: bone attachments | Rig and clips per body plan | Pilot for the knight only |
| 3D pre-rendered to sprites | High | As 3D | Weak: a bake per item and frame | Rig, clips and bake | Rejected |
| Painterly 2D foreground | Medium (muddy values) | Medium: hard to normalise | Good with a cut-out rig | One illustration | Backdrops only |
| HD-2D or lit hi-bit pixel | Medium to high | Low: every asset serves the lighting | Medium | High | Rejected |

No game shows anything like 149 slots on a body, and LootFall shouldn't try. Riot's clarity rule is that a character keeps one primary trait recognisable "in darkness", with facing readable at a glance ([Riot](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/ask-riot-let-s-talk-clarity/)); for the knight that means gear changes shape only within a fixed envelope per slot, so he always reads as the knight. About ten worn slots become cut-out attachments (main hand, off hand, helm, breastplate, tabard or tunic, cloak, near pauldron, near gauntlet, belt and boots), each authored in neutral colours with a trim mask that code tints in its rarity colour, which generalises today's blade and cape palette swap ([CLAUDE.md](/home/user/lootfall/CLAUDE.md)). The jewellery, tattoo, trinket and accessory slots show as aggregate effects, such as glints on the sword hand whose colour follows the best ring, or sigils that flare on a crit; companions walk beside him as small actors; and the sixteen celestial slots, already named Halo, Wings, Aura, Starcrown, Comet, Nebula, Eclipse, Zenith and eight constellations ([Content.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Progression/Content.cs)), become shader and particle layers, so the late game's visible power costs effects, not paintings. Players do expect earned items to show, as IdleOn's "not visible on character" complaint shows ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1476970/discussions/0/3073118388428222324)), which is why mobile-style costume skins would break LootFall's promise that gear shows up in the fight.

The case rests on reasoning, not measurement, because no public source reports hours per asset for any of these pipelines. Before production, time one knight, ten gear pieces and three monster body plans (a biped, a quadruped, and a flyer or blob) end to end, through Godot import and a bot screenshot run. The backdrops, icons, UI, VFX and rarity layers are identical whichever way the knight is rendered, so if the pilot shows a real-time toon knight with bone-attached gear working under GL Compatibility, he can replace the cut-out knight without touching anything else; the monsters stay 2D either way.

## One knight in one lane, scaled by four presentation modes

The side-view lane is the established format for idle combat with a single hero, and LootFall should keep it, knight fixed at x 300 as the one focal point. Clicker Heroes built its pacing on a boss every five zones that must die within **30 seconds** ([Clicker Heroes blog](https://blog.clickerheroes.com/clicker-heroes-monsters-ultimate-guide-to-enemies-strategies/)), which turns abstract power into a visible pass-or-fail test, and Tap Titans 2 shows outgrown content through stage skips ([r/TapTitans2 guide](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/TapTitans2/wiki/newplayerguide)). The alternatives show power through the very thing their players complain about. Late Vampire Survivors "often turns into a black screen and/or screen of rainbows filled with damage numbers" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1794680/discussions/0/5287792084457285105)); isometric ARPGs need loot filters and a free camera to stay readable; party formations split attention away from the one hero; and card or diorama formats lose the physical fall of chests out of monsters, while Loop Hero drew the complaint that "we don't see any attacks connect" ([Geeks Under Grace](https://www.geeksundergrace.com/gaming/pc/review-loop-hero/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/loop-hero-review)).

Combat itself should stay automatic and deterministic in Core, as it is now: the battle steps in slices, reports events, and the screen animates them without deciding anything ([CLAUDE.md](/home/user/lootfall/CLAUDE.md)). The player's verbs are gear, chests and the boss gate (today a boss after ten kills and a guardian at the end of each place ([design.md](/home/user/lootfall/docs/design.md))), so the presentation's job is to make every outcome explainable. Bosses should telegraph their heavy attack with a wind-up pose and an intent icon, as Slay the Spire's intents show an attack's damage before it lands ([untapped.gg](https://sts2.untapped.gg/guides/how-to-read-enemy-intent)) and Brotato's chargers "give a few seconds warning" ([Games Asylum](https://www.gamesasylum.com/2024/02/06/brotato-review/); [VideoChums](https://videochums.com/article/brotato-vs-vampire-survivors)). The knight's attacks should stay in one hue family and monster threats in another, the distinction Path of Exile 2 players miss when friendly and hostile ground effects look alike ([PoE forum](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3598854)). Fast fights need speed control and a readable account afterwards, which a Backpack Battles player asked for as "a log replay feature" ([itch.io](https://playwithfurcifer.itch.io/backpack-battles)); a short log of what downed the knight and what dropped covers it.

Growth over thousands of hours should change how fights are shown, not pile more on screen. The switch follows time-to-kill rather than depth, so the presentation always matches what the knight is actually doing:

| Mode | When | What the lane shows | Numbers | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Duel | A monster takes a second or more | Every swing with anticipation and impact; flinch, knockback, a puff and coins on a kill | One per hit (16, 24, 32 px) | Crit stop 30-50 ms, at most every 0.5 s |
| Wave | Several kills a second | Monsters arrive in groups; one arc fells several | Merged per swing into one total, plus a kill tally ("x12") | Hit-stop only for bosses and rare events; shake per kill capped low |
| Sweep | More than one depth a second | Monsters fall on contact as the knight runs; parallax speeds up; the depth counter rolls | One readout: depths per minute, or "1-hit" | Chests rain into the pile as counts; only rare-or-better and first finds pop |
| Leap | Many depths a second | A fast-forward cut that eases the time scale in and out | A banner: "Depth 12,400 to 12,950, 3 legendaries" | Best finds staged afterwards |

In every mode the boss gate stops the sweep and plays at full strength, and a boss killed in one blow gets a stinger (about 100 to 150 ms of hit-stop, a flash and its own sound) that marks the moment it was outgrown. A fixed on-screen budget, proposed at **8 to 12 monsters and 6 to 10 floating numbers** whatever the power level, makes growth show as speed and reach rather than density. These thresholds are untested proposals to check with the bot's `--shots` and `--write-movie` captures at high `--stage` values.

LootFall's feel numbers already sit inside the published bands; the risk is in how they extrapolate. Celeste freezes for **0.05 s** when a dash starts and **0.1 s** on a bumper launch ([Celeste Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)); a 2024 study rated hit-stops of **0.1 to 0.2 s** best for blows that send enemies flying ([SciTePress](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2024/124614/124614.pdf)); and Smash scales hitlag with damage up to a cap of 20 frames in Melee and 30 from Brawl on ([SmashWiki](https://ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)). LootFall's crit stop of 0.03 s and boss kill of 0.12 s fit ([BattleView.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Ui/BattleView.cs)), and its shake already uses trauma squared in the manner of Squirrel Eiserloh's GDC talk ([GameDev.tv forum](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/enhanced-camera-shake-with-perlin-noise/204230); [Main.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Main.cs)). But the chest-opening formulas (hit-stop of 0.08 s at legendary plus 0.06 s per tier beyond it; flash alpha of 0.06 plus 0.06 per tier) reach **0.5 s and 0.72 alpha at the twelfth tier** ([ChestPanel.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Ui/ChestPanel.cs)), Smash's ceiling and a near-white screen, so both need caps or flatter growth. The hit-stop also works by setting `Engine.TimeScale`, which slows UI tweens and the chest panel too; practitioner guides freeze only the attacker and target, because a global pause freezes UI, audio and effects and "feels wrong" ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-hitstop-or-freeze-frame-feeling-off); [uhiyama-lab](https://uhiyama-lab.com/en/notes/unity/unity-game-feel-hit-feedback/)), and Juicy Breakout's freezer eases speed down and back up instead of snapping ([Freezer.as](https://github.com/grapefrukt/juicy-breakout/blob/master/src/com/grapefrukt/games/juicy/Freezer.as)). That demo's definition of juice, "cascading action and response for minimal user input" ([juicy-breakout](https://github.com/grapefrukt/juicy-breakout)), fits an idle game exactly: with almost no input, the cascade has to run from the game's own events, kill to coins to chest pop to pile.

Numbers need the same restraint, because monster health reaches about **1e830 by depth 20,000** ([Big.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Progression/Big.cs)). Antimatter Dimensions offers 21 notations and defaults to mixed scientific ([notations](https://github.com/antimatter-dimensions/notations); [AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode)), which is what LootFall's formatter already prints, so the missing piece is a notation setting applied on every screen. World of Warcraft's 2014 stat squish shows what players actually read: a Fireball dealing 450,000 to a 3,000,000-health enemy became 30,000 against 200,000, still 15% ([PC Invasion](https://pcinvasion.com/world-of-warcraft-warlords-of-draenor-patch-notes-reveal-stat-squishing)). Relative readouts (hits to kill, the boss timer's margin, depths per minute, power in LE) survive any notation. Floating numbers should merge per target per swing ([Damage Sparks](https://thunderstore.io/c/sulfur/p/ryuka_labs/DamageSparks/v/1.0.12/)), stay in one region at a smaller size, as Neverwinter confined them to the enemy's upper-right quadrant and cut them by 23 to 50% ([Neverwinter dev post](https://forum.arcgames.com/neverwinter/roletracker/jump/1266779)), and make crits bigger and steadier rather than only recoloured. Clutter controls belong in Settings from launch: Halls of Torment players say its effect opacity slider "alone makes a gigantic difference" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/discussions/0/3957035999325195508)), and Vampire Survivors gives damage numbers, flashing effects and shake separate switches ([VS wiki](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Options)), whereas LootFall today scales flash and shake with one setting.

The lootfall itself must stay visible: a chest should be seen leaving the monster that dropped it, even late, when drops are counted rather than shown one by one, because that link between fight and loot is the game's identity. Time away needs a presentation to match its existing report (75% of the live rate for up to 8 hours, at most 100 chests held ([Balance.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Core/Balance.cs))): an Antimatter Dimensions-style "While you were away for..." card with before and after values, the cap stated, an honest "Nothing happened." when true ([AwayProgressModal.vue](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/components/modals/AwayProgressModal.vue)), and the best one to three finds staged as reveals rather than listed. For players who leave the game running, an optional compact strip view in the spirit of Rusty's Retirement, which lives in a bar along the bottom of the desktop ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-rustys-retirement-idle-farmed)), would suit ambient play.

## Code, not the generator, must carry twelve rarities and 149 slots

Every large loot interface found keeps rarity out of the icon art. World of Warcraft tints one shared white frame texture with the quality colour and never recolours the icon, and marks upgrades and new items with separate overlays, a green arrow and a pulse ([wow-ui-source](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source)); commercial icon packs sell their rarity borders as a separate layer ([Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/cc12e99f-03e7-48db-95af-279bcd39bdb9); [itch.io pack](https://uiuxforge.itch.io/bold-icons-pack)). LootFall should build each icon from three layers: neutral base art per item family, a rarity frame and background drawn by code in twelve variants, and overlays for state (upgrade arrow, new pulse, first-find badge, affix dots, rank stars, tier numeral), drawn as sprites because the font is ASCII-only. If the art escalates at all, it escalates in material and ornament (iron, steel, gilt, gem-set, runic), never in hue, so the colour channel stays unambiguous. That one decision removes AI's hardest consistency problem: no generator ever has to paint twelve rarity versions of anything.

Colour can't carry twelve tiers alone. The shared vocabulary is five hues in a fixed order, grey or white, green, blue, purple and orange, as World of Warcraft set them (Uncommon #1eff00, Rare #0070dd, Epic #a335ee, Legendary #ff8000) ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Quality)); Grim Dawn departed from it and earned a forum thread of confusion ([Crate forum](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/why-did-grim-dawn-change-the-usual-arpg-item-colors/157478)); and with many tiers Terraria warns that some rarity colours "are easily confused, e.g. Green/Lime, Orange/Light Red" ([Terraria Wiki](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Rarity)). LootFall's first five names match WoW's and should take its colours. Tiers six to nine have precedents: red, as in Last Epoch's Legendary ([Upcomer](https://upcomer.com/last-epoch-loot-rarity-colors-explained)); pink, as in Guild Wars 2's Ascended ([MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/?p=407107)); cyan, as in Borderlands' pearlescent ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=78526)); and light gold, as in WoW's Artifact. The last three should differ by treatment, the way NeverSink's filter inverts text and background for its top tier ([NeverSink-Filter](https://github.com/NeverSinkDev/NeverSink-Filter)). Grouping the ladder into four bands gives every tier a non-colour cue (frame class plus numeral), and the same bands set the opening's choreography:

| Band | Tiers (colour proposal) | Frame and non-colour cue | Opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | I Common (white or light grey), II Uncommon (#1eff00), III Rare (#0070dd) | Plain frame, numeral | Card colour and a soft sound; commons take half a second |
| B | IV Epic (#a335ee), V Legendary (#ff8000) | Ornate frame | A beam and a distinct sound |
| C | VI Mythic (red), VII Ascended (pink or magenta), VIII Celestial (cyan, #00ccff) | Crowned frame, animated edge | Flash and shake through `Main` |
| D | IX Divine (light gold, #e6cc80), X Primordial (dark background, coloured glow), XI Eternal (white or silver background, coloured edge), XII Transcendent (animated prismatic) | Radiant frame, particles | A held reveal with its own sound |

The bands follow the game's own structure, since Ascended needs an ascension and each tier after it one more transcendence ([Content.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Progression/Content.cs)). Borderlands' Randy Pitchford says not every player will ever see a pearlescent ([Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/updates/id/556679/article/randy-pitchford-on-borderlands-4-rarity-not-all-players-will-ever-see-a-pearlescent/)), and band D should feel that rare; Hearthstone's face-down cards glow in their rarity's colour before the flip ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Card_packs)), a pre-reveal that suits it better than ever-longer freezes. The palette is a proposal that still needs a colour-vision-deficiency simulation, and it should ship with the safety nets players ask for: the tier's name and numeral in text (Borderlands 2's colourblind mode added colour names to weapon descriptions ([Co-Optimus](https://www.co-optimus.com/article/11338/borderlands-2-to-receive-colorblind-mode.html))) and per-rarity colour overrides, which WoW exposes in its settings ([wow-ui-source](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source)). Two rules keep the ladder meaningful for thousands of hours. Reserve the top signals strictly: Diablo III removed the legendary beam from Greater Rift keys once it fired for something unimportant ([Softpedia](https://games.softpedia.com/blog/Next-Diablo-3-Patch-Removes-Legendary-Beam-from-Greater-Rift-Keys-458391.shtml)). And separate what an item is from how much it matters now, as NeverSink and RuneLite grade value inside one hue ([RuneLite](https://github.com/runelite/runelite/blob/master/runelite-client/src/main/java/net/runelite/client/plugins/grounditems/GroundItemsConfig.java)): hue is fixed per rarity, while emphasis (size, beam, sound, pulse) goes to upgrades and first finds, so a Rare upgrade can shout louder than a Divine the knight can't use.

The paper doll stops scaling at about twenty slots; WoW's 18 buttons in two columns and a weapon row already fill its pane ([wow-ui-source](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source)). For 149, the armoury needs two levels: an overview of the eight slot kinds, each tile showing slots open out of total, the best rarity and a count of available upgrades, and a grid per kind clustered by body region, with a hand cluster for the ten rings and a foot cluster for the ten toe rings. A full-screen armoury at a 56 px pitch holds about 22 by 12 cells, room for every open slot, while the strip under the lane can't hold 149 icons even at 48 px. Only open slots and the next few to open should be drawn, following Path of Exile's Unique Collection Tab, which gives unobtainable uniques no slot until one is found ([Bex_GGG](https://devtrackers.gg/pathofexile/p/3de9e1f6-introducing-the-unique-collection-tab)); LootFall's data already opens the eight classic slots first and the celestial slots last ([Content.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Progression/Content.cs)). Forge, rank and mastery belong on the slot frame as pips or stars, the way Legends of Idleon ranks cards from 0 to 6 stars ([DigitalTQ](https://www.digitaltq.com/wiki/idleon/items/cards/sacrilegious-kattlekruk-card-1782)). Each item should carry one verdict, "+1.4 LE" with an arrow, as Path of Building makes "how much of an upgrade a given item is" a first-class number ([PathOfBuilding](https://github.com/PathOfBuildingCommunity/PathOfBuilding)), with Diablo IV-style gained and lost lines on demand ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/diablo-4-best-hidden-setting-compare-gear)) behind a held key other than Space or Enter, which belong to the chest, and one press should equip every upgrade. There should be no bag to manage: unopened chests show as counts by kind, held finds as a short queue with verdicts, and Auto-Open's filter copies NeverSink's strictness presets over readable rules ([NeverSink-Filter](https://github.com/NeverSinkDev/NeverSink-Filter)). It should record which rule decided each item, the fix Last Epoch's "Terrible Tooltips" mod added after players mistook rule behaviour for a bug ([Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/lastepoch/mods/30); [Last Epoch forum](https://forum.lastepoch.com/t/loot-filter-help-unsure-if-bug/70656)), preview what a rule would have kept, and protect upgrades and first finds by default, as WoW's EasySell protects soulbound items ([CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/easysell)), with a short undo list for anything salvaged.

The 3,259-node talent tree is the size of Path of Exile's, whose official export has 2,357 connected main-tree nodes plus 558 ascendancy records ([skilltree-export](https://github.com/grindinggear/skilltree-export)), so PoE's visual grammar is the proven template. Size gives class: small, notable, keystone and mastery icons at 34, 49, 68 and 113 px, in frames of 51, 76 and 109 px, roughly 1 : 1.5 : 2.1. Frames take three states (unallocated, can allocate, allocated), connectors two, and the sprite sheets span a 4x zoom range ([skilltree-export](https://github.com/grindinggear/skilltree-export)). LootFall's tree already has the radial skeleton for it, with six sectors (Blade, Bulwark, Gale, Blood, Fortune, Ruin), fourteen rings and eighteen keystones ([Talents.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Progression/Talents.cs)), so each sector can be a colour-coded wedge with notables where the rings meet. Guides to PoE 2 call its tree overwhelming until players learn it ([Vulkk](https://vulkk.com/2025/02/08/path-of-exile-2-passive-tree-guide/)), and the answers are tools: a search that makes matches flash ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mmo/path-exile-2-poe2-passive-skill-filter-search-skill)), Path of Building's shift-click path tracing and its "Show Node Power" heat map with a capped search depth ([TreeTab.lua](https://github.com/PathOfBuildingCommunity/PathOfBuilding/blob/dev/src/Classes/TreeTab.lua)), zoom levels that show only sectors, keystones and notables from far out, and a "planned" overlay distinct from "allocated". Because a PoE build takes only about 5% of its tree, the screen should open on a summary of bonuses by stat family with `Policy`'s suggested next node highlighted, leaving the full map optional for idle players.

The codex is the largest collection UI anyone has attempted: **114,432 entries**, 149 slots by 12 rarities by 4 quality bands by 16 eras ([Hero.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Progression/Hero.cs)), about 60 times Old School RuneScape's 1,925-slot collection log ([OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Collection_log)). It has to aggregate: an overview of the eight kinds with completion bars and a rarity-by-era heat map; a kind page with one row per slot and a 12-segment rarity bar; and a slot page with a 12 by 64 grid of tiny cells, 96 by 512 px at 8 px a cell. Unreachable eras and rarities should stay hidden, or the first screen reads "0.01% complete". New entries should be batched into one line per chest with a pulse on the tab, since even OSRS lets players switch off its per-entry message ([RuneLite ScreenshotPlugin](https://github.com/runelite/runelite/blob/master/runelite-client/src/main/java/net/runelite/client/plugins/screenshot/ScreenshotPlugin.java)), with full popups kept for milestones (first of a rarity, a slot or an era complete), and Diablo IV's codex filters and "where it drops" hovers turn the grid into a goal-finder ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/d4/guides/legendary-aspects-codex-of-power-guide/); [Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2023/04/24/diablo-4-codex-power-legendary-aspects/)). With this many systems (gear, chests, forge, ranks, mastery, talents, codex, two prestige layers), Antimatter Dimensions' structure is the model: 11 top-level tabs and 42 subtabs, each appearing only when its condition unlocks, 49 of the 53 hidable, a one-time highlight on unlock, a 48-entry How-to-Play reference, and progress bars filled on a log scale toward a named goal ([AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode)). The gating belongs in Core, so the sim, the fast-forward and the bot see the same unlocks.

## About 85 art families and four rules make the AI pipeline tractable

The pipeline is built for what AI does reliably, not its best case, and four rules make it work. Generated art is neutral and static: no rarity colour, glow, background, text or motion is ever asked of a model. Assets come in families: a base design per archetype, with variants made by multi-reference editing and palette-ramp swaps rather than independent generations, the "same creature, new biome" pattern LootFall's ember, ash, shade and gilded reskins already follow. Output is keyed, never trusted for alpha: render on a flat key colour or as a white and black pair and matte in the repo, so the next provider change costs nothing. Acceptance is scripted: a cleanup tool in the repo, in the spirit of `tools/import_art.py`, normalises outline width and palette ramps, crops and centres, checks bounding-box fill, the brightest quadrant (light direction) and legibility at display size, and writes a contact sheet, after which the bot's screenshots judge everything in context. Generation runs wherever the owner runs the models, on their own machine or in a GitHub Actions workflow with API keys kept as secrets, because cloud sessions reach only GitHub ([CLAUDE.md](/home/user/lootfall/CLAUDE.md)); everything after generation runs in the repo, where a session can verify it. A style bible of **20 to 30 approved images** anchors it all, within the 10 to 30 that custom-model training wants ([Layer](https://help.layer.ai/en/articles/14094114-how-to-train-a-custom-model-lora)), serving both as training data and as the reference set for every prompt.

| Asset | How many | Generation target | Processing in the repo | On screen (1280x720 layout) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Style bible | 20-30 anchors: knight, three monsters, ten icons, two backdrops, one UI concept | Approved by hand, then frozen | Reference set and training data | Not shown |
| Item icons | About 85 families x 4-6 material tiers, roughly 350-500 | Native 1-2K on a key colour or white/black pair; fixed three-quarter view and angle; top-left light; one dark outline; object inside an 80% safe box; neutral materials; no rarity colour, glow, background or text | Matte, crop, centre, outline and palette normalised, checks run; 256 px master, imported at the largest size shown | 64 px armoury, 80 px reveal card, 48 px dense grids; legibility checked at 32 px |
| Knight | One character, about 20 parts | Painted once in a neutral side-view pose facing right, limbs clear, as a parts sheet; human paint-over | Parts manifest with pivots (like today's `actors.json` foot points); rig and clips built in code | About 170-200 px tall, authored at 2x |
| Worn gear | About 10 visible slot types x 3-5 shapes x material tiers | Each piece in its worn orientation on a pivot template, neutral colours, a marked trim region | Trim mask extracted, pivot aligned, shape checked against the slot's envelope | On the knight |
| Monsters | Dozens of archetypes, each with biome variants | One side-view illustration facing left at the largest size it appears (boss scale) at 2x, tagged with a body plan; bosses also split into 2-6 parts | Matte, outline and palette checks; variants by reference editing plus palette ramps | About 90-160 px; bosses up to 3x |
| Backdrops | 3-4 parallax layers per biome | Each layer generated separately on a key colour, horizontally tileable; near layer 2560x840, far layers at half that | Value and saturation clamped below the actors' by script; seam check; re-grades for darker revisits | The 1280x420 lane |
| VFX elements | 30-60 textures | Single frames on black (sparks, slashes, glows, puffs, rings, runes), 256-512 px greyscale | Additive blend, tinted by a rarity uniform, moved by Godot particles and shaders that run under GL Compatibility | Lane overlays |
| UI | Frames, panels, buttons | Not generated; at most ornament fills cut from one whole-screen concept | 9-slice margins marked by hand; tested tall, wide and at minimum size | Everywhere |
| Guardian portraits, store art | One per guardian, plus the store page | Frontier-model illustration with human paint-over; store capsule commissioned or heavily repainted | None | Boss intros, reveal cards, Steam |

The family count comes straight from the slot list. Of the 149 slots ([Content.cs](/home/user/lootfall/scripts/Progression/Content.cs)), left and right pieces mirror one design, the twenty finger and toe rings share two families, and the 24 trinkets (Charm, Relic, Talisman and Idol, six of each) share four, which leaves **about 85 base families**. At four to six material tiers tracking the codex's sixteen eras, that makes a few hundred icons, a set one person can keep coherent with every visual variable locked and enforced, where thousands of independent generations would not be. Money is not the constraint. Even 3,000 icons at four attempts each, 12,000 images at list prices of $0.035 to $0.08 ([HowAIWorks](https://howaiworks.ai/models/seedream); [Segmind](https://www.segmind.com/models/recraft-v4-pro-svg)), costs roughly **$420 to $960**, but accepting or rejecting each in ten seconds is about **33 hours of review**, so human curation sets the budget and every check a script takes over is time returned.

A few assets deserve a human hand: the knight's parts, the guardians, and above all the store capsule, since a PC Gamer column calls AI-generated capsule art spam that makes browsing Steam less fun ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-spammy-ai-generated-capsule-art-is-a-pox-and-it-makes-browsing-steam-less-fun/)). Human edits matter for ownership too: the US Supreme Court declined Thaler v. Perlmutter on 2 March 2026, leaving human authorship required, and the Copyright Office holds that prompts alone aren't enough ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case)). The move to illustrated art also changes project settings. The canvas default is nearest filtering with pixel-snapped transforms, and the window stretches in `canvas_items` mode ([project.godot](/home/user/lootfall/project.godot)), so the new layers need linear filtering with mipmaps and art authored at 2x the 1280x720 layout to stay crisp at 1440p, and the pixel font Kenney Mini should give way to vector families from Google Fonts, a fantasy serif such as Cinzel for headers and a condensed face such as Oswald or Bebas Neue with fixed-width figures for numbers, which can be final rather than placeholders. Godot's cut-out tools (Skeleton2D, Polygon2D, AnimationTree) cover the knight, though its 2D IK works only in the editor ([godot-docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot-docs/master/tutorials/animation/cutout_animation.rst)), one more reason to build the rig and its clips in code, the way the UI already is.

Because every placeholder will be replaced, the test for one is how well it stages the target look and exercises the new code paths, not its licence. Painted, static creatures are the most valuable find, since a still painting moved by procedural motion is the production pipeline in miniature.

GitHub's REST and search APIs and its archive downloads refuse requests from these sessions, but git over HTTPS works: a blobless sparse checkout (`git clone --depth=1 --filter=blob:none --sparse`, then `git sparse-checkout set` on the folders needed) fetched 29 full clones and 9 sparse checkouts in 43 seconds during this research, and brings Wesnoth's portraits down to 32 MB instead of a full clone. ## Conclusion

The art question turned out to be an information-design question. Once everything players must read the same way every time (rarity, slot state, motion, frames, numbers) lives in code, AI's characteristic failures (drift across a set, flicker across frames, missing alpha) stop touching the information that matters, and the generator is left with the job it does best: neutral stills in families. The same split makes the art replaceable, because families can be regenerated as models improve without touching rarity, animation or UI, so the look can rise with the tools instead of freezing at late-2026 quality. And it is what keeps a mostly AI-made game from looking like one: coherence is enforced by scripts rather than hoped for from prompts.

Over thousands of hours the threat to clarity and satisfaction is clutter and sameness, not low fidelity, so restraint belongs inside the art direction: a fixed on-screen budget, top signals reserved for tiers most players rarely see, emphasis that follows relevance rather than tier, and presentation modes that turn growth into speed. The 2D-or-3D question is also narrower than it looks. Only the knight could repay 3D, the monsters decide the cost, and the choice can wait for a timed pilot without holding anything else up, because the layers that carry the game's readability are 2D either way.
